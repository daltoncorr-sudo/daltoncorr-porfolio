Dear Sundance Institute team,

A festival's identity lives at building scale. For the past four years I have designed the
physical world of the Oscar-qualifying HollyShorts Film Festival — the step-and-repeats and red
carpets, the billboards over Hollywood, the photo walls, banners, and venue signage that turn TCL
Chinese Theatres, the Egyptian Theatre, and Japan House into one continuous place. As Sundance
builds its first festival footprint in Boulder, I'd like to help you design that sense of place
as your Festival Environmental Graphic Designer.

As art director of the 19th, 20th, and 21st HollyShorts editions, environmental graphics were
mine end to end: concept, design, print production, fabrication, and installation across multiple
venues opening simultaneously — plus the production design of the festival's Award Show, staged
live from the Egyptian Theatre, and of the Alta Global Media Film Summit. I work directly with
print and fabrication partners, hold color and quality at large format, and problem-solve on-site
when a wall, a window, or a deadline refuses to cooperate. The campaigns this environment work
belonged to were covered by Variety, Deadline, Daily Mail, and TimeOut, and the posters that
anchored them were named among the world's best film festival posters by FestivalsInLA in 2023
and 2024.

What makes Boulder's first edition special is also what makes it hard: the graphics must teach a
new city what Sundance feels like, and teach Sundance audiences their way around a new city —
identity and wayfinding at once. I've done this launch before. In 2025 I was creative director of
the inaugural HollyShorts London, building a city-specific visual system inside an established
parent brand, from screen graphics down to street-level presence. I also led the rebrand of the
Hollywood Comedy Shorts Film Festival into HollyShorts Comedy, a full design system that ran from
print and badges through interactive AR activations at the venue.

Sundance is personal for me: I'm a working film composer as well as a designer — my work on the
documentary *Alchemy* was part of Sundance Collab in 2019 — so I design festival spaces knowing
what that walk to the theater means to a filmmaker.

I'm based between Los Angeles and New York, both listed locations for this role, and fully
available September 2, 2026 through January 26, 2027, including travel to Boulder and festival
hours. I'd love to help audiences step off the street in Boulder and know, immediately, that they
have arrived at Sundance.

Thank you for your consideration.

Dalton Corr
